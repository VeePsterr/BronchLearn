# Lesson: orienting yourself in the airway

<ReviewBadge status="source-verified" /> Branch names checked against FIPAT TA2 (AI-assisted, not a human review).

This lesson follows a simple teaching order: start at the trachea, find the carina, decide right versus left, then move to the lobar and segmental bronchi. The illustrations on this site are schematic and do not show what you will see through a real bronchoscope.

## 1. Start at the trachea

Every route on this site begins at the **trachea**. At its lower end the trachea divides (the *tracheal bifurcation*). The ridge between the two openings is the **carina of the trachea**, often called the main carina.

## 2. Establish right versus left

Each side has its own sequence of branches. Learning which branches belong to which side is the most reliable way to stay oriented.

- The **right main bronchus** gives the **right superior lobar bronchus** and continues as the **intermediate bronchus** (bronchus intermedius).
- The **left main bronchus** gives the **left superior lobar bronchus** and the **left inferior lobar bronchus**.

Always confirm side with explicit labels. On this site, R and L are written out and never shown by colour alone.

## 3. Lobar bronchi

| Side | Lobar bronchus | Parent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Right | Right superior lobar bronchus | Right main bronchus |
| Right | Middle lobar bronchus | Intermediate bronchus |
| Right | Right inferior lobar bronchus | Intermediate bronchus |
| Left | Left superior lobar bronchus | Left main bronchus |
| Left | Left inferior lobar bronchus | Left main bronchus |

## 4. Segmental bronchi

- **Right upper lobe:** RB1 apical, RB2 posterior, RB3 anterior
- **Right middle lobe:** RB4 lateral, RB5 medial
- **Right lower lobe:** RB6 superior, RB7 medial basal, RB8 anterior basal, RB9 lateral basal, RB10 posterior basal
- **Left upper lobe:** LB1+2 apicoposterior, LB3 anterior, LB4 superior lingular, LB5 inferior lingular
- **Left lower lobe:** LB6 superior, LB7 medial basal and LB8 anterior basal when separately present/described, or LB7+8 anteromedial basal when represented as a combined branch, then LB9 lateral basal and LB10 posterior basal

The combined patterns on the left (LB1+2, and LB7+8 as an alternative) are nomenclature and anatomical variants. They are not mistakes.
